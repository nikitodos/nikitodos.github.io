Technical Report

XSLR™: Architecture, Thermodynamic Analysis, and Scaling of a Dual-Loop Hybrid Electric Expander Cycle Linear Aerospike Engine for Multi-Mission Propulsion

Independent research — Part of the Space Framework · March - June 2026

Consolidated thermodynamic and architectural study of the eXperimental aeroSpike Liquid Rocket (XSLR™), the chemical propulsion subsystem of the Skyraker™ hybrid propulsion system, the heart of Ikarus™. Starting from a structured trade study of four dual-loop cooling configurations for a linear aerospike engine derived from the Rocketdyne XRS-2200, the analysis selects a parallel full-expander architecture augmented by a hybrid Electric Expander Cycle (EEC) as the recommended baseline across all thrust classes from 22 to 250 kN. This replaces the spike-loop LH2 turbopump with a BLDC motor-driven centrifugal pump, enabling 5:1 throttle, combined cell shutdown and cold-start without pyrotechnic igniters.

Last updated: June 2026

Application of the Karhunen-Loève Transform to the Search for Extraterrestrial Telemetry Signals

Supervisors: Prof. Giovanni Covone, Dott. Matteo Trudu, Dott. Andrea Melis · May 16th, 2025

B.Sc. thesis in Physics. Applied the Karhunen–Loève Transform (KLT) to datasets from the Sardinia Radio Telescope within the Breakthrough Listen programme. Validated the pipeline on Voyager 1 as a known artificial source; applied to TOI-1422 b, a Neptune-mass exoplanet in the habitable zone of a solar-type star. Results show significant RFI reduction and improved detection of weak technosignatures, following the theoretical framework of Claudio Maccone.

Defended: May 16th, 2025

The Space Framework

A long-range concept for integrating the resources of the Solar System into the terrestrial economy — accelerating the transition to a space-oriented industrial model while improving the safety and flexibility of human spaceflight.

Three pillars: REDSTAR modular stations (6,400 m³ — 8× ISS), the Spectre multipurpose spacecraft (8 crew + 150 t payload, autonomous interplanetary flight), and in-situ resource utilization across Solar System bodies.

The X-RAYJAR fusion reactor exploits X-ray-induced fusion in lithium deuteride crystals — following Belyaev et al. (2016, Phys. Rev. C 93, 034622) — as the energy backbone for deep-space operations.

The legal dimension is confronted directly: the Outer Space Treaty of 1967 cannot govern kinetic weapons, resource extraction, or the Spacepower doctrine now codified by the U.S. Space Force. The Framework proposes a European-led architecture for what comes next.
